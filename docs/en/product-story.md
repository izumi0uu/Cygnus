# Support Brain for SaaS — Complete Product Story

## 1. What this document is for
This is not a PRD rewrite and not an expanded IA doc.

It answers:
- who the protagonist is in the complete product narrative,
- what ongoing tension this product is built to resolve,
- why the product must feel like a control tower rather than an agent workspace or generic agent tool,
- how supporting surfaces should exist around that center.

## 2. Core thesis
**Support Brain for SaaS** is not primarily about making AI “better at acting like support.”

Its larger thesis is:
**give support leaders a way to see, judge, coordinate, and change a support system that is drifting out of sync.**

That system includes:
- docs and help center content,
- internal SOPs and internal knowledge,
- recurring ticket and chat patterns,
- release / incident / known-issue movement,
- audience differences across plans, regions, and versions,
- the answers actually being consumed by copilots and humans.

## 3. Who the protagonist is
The protagonist is not:
- the frontline agent,
- a single AI copilot,
- a customer-facing bot,
- a workflow that drafts answers.

The protagonist is:
**the support leader / support ops / knowledge manager decision surface.**

The first frame of the product should therefore begin with **where the support system is losing alignment**, not with a single agent handling a single ticket.

## 4. What this protagonist is fighting
The enemy is not simply “not enough knowledge.”
It is deeper misalignment:

1. **Knowledge is out of sync with reality**
   - docs are stale while releases already changed,
   - known issues escalated while the copilot still serves old answers.

2. **The same answer is wrong for different audiences**
   - free, enterprise, EU, and legacy-version users should not get the same response.

3. **The same problem erupts in different places without a global view**
   - tickets flare,
   - chatbot traffic flares,
   - help center coverage is absent,
   - leadership sees only fragments.

4. **The support system is consuming human effort without a command layer**
   - agents keep rewriting answers,
   - escalations rise,
   - AI confidence drops,
   - but nobody knows which intervention has the highest leverage first.

## 5. Why it must be a control tower
If the product is narrated as a shared human+AI workspace, two failures happen.

### 5.1 The center of gravity shifts to execution instead of judgment
It starts to feel like:
- an answer suggestion tool,
- a conversation assistance tool,
- a ticket handling efficiency tool.

Instead of:
- a prioritization tool,
- a knowledge-governance tool,
- a support-system coordination tool.

### 5.2 Global capabilities get diluted
Once the narrative center is wrong, these capabilities weaken:
- global prioritization,
- risk warning,
- cross-queue coordination,
- cross-team routing,
- publish impact judgment,
- audience impact judgment.

## 6. The five-act structure of the complete story

### Act 1: See the system move
Users do not arrive to “open an object.”
They first see:
- topics heating up,
- queue pressure changing,
- answers drifting,
- audiences being mismatched,
- objects becoming risk centers.

This is not a static document homepage.
It is an entry point into **what the support system is doing right now**.

### Act 2: Understand what matters now
The product should not simply reveal more data.
It should help answer:
**if I can intervene in only one thing right now, where should I move first?**

That means composing:
- blast radius,
- affected audiences,
- implicated knowledge objects,
- links to release / incident / policy movement,
- current owner and current status,
- whether downstream surfaces are already contaminated.

### Act 3: Coordinate and command
This is the dividing line between the product and a conventional dashboard.
The user does not just “see a problem.” They issue system-shaping moves:
- route a cluster to a knowledge manager,
- push a policy rule into urgent review,
- pause external publication of a risky answer,
- prioritize a specific audience mismatch,
- redirect the support organization through a different escalation route.

The main feeling is:
**I am commanding a distributed support system back into alignment.**

### Act 4: Propagate through supporting surfaces
The control tower is not the endpoint.
Its commands change:
- review queue priority,
- knowledge object sequencing,
- publish rule openness or restriction,
- copilot answer suggestions,
- exposure of customer-facing answer surfaces.

The story must make this propagation visible.
Otherwise the product becomes just another dashboard.

### Act 5: See whether the system realigned
The product must answer not “did an action fire?” but:
**did the support system become safer, clearer, and more aligned because of that action?**

So it should reflect:
- whether rewrites fall,
- whether escalations settle,
- whether drift converges,
- whether coverage closes,
- whether downstream answer behavior re-aligns.

## 7. The role of supporting surfaces

### Agent copilot surface
Not the protagonist.
It exists to validate whether governed knowledge is usable and whether frontline rewriting still persists.

### Human support surface
Also not the protagonist.
It is where control-tower decisions shape frontline work.

### Customer-facing answer surface
Important, but downstream.
It should not steal the control-layer narrative.

## 8. Four high-value scenarios
- **Release drift**: new release, stale docs, rising volume.
- **Incident spread**: multiple queues and languages start mis-answering the same issue.
- **Policy change collision**: one rule change causes audience-specific inconsistency.
- **Copilot rewrite drag**: humans keep correcting “working” AI, revealing a hidden governance problem.

## 9. Required narrative feeling
The product should not feel like:
- a smarter document backend,
- an AI approval console,
- a support efficiency plugin.

It should feel like:
- support mission control,
- a place where system-level movement becomes visible,
- a place for command rather than chat.

## 10. Where this story lands
This product story exists to anchor three downstream artifacts:
1. frontend behavior story,
2. visual language system,
3. interaction principles.

It must keep one boundary intact:
**Cygnus / Support Brain is the control layer of a support knowledge operating system, not an agent workflow desktop.**
